Buying a smart apartment: what to check.

“Smart home” in a listing can mean a few smart plugs or a full automation system built into the building. Before you buy, ask what is installed, who operates it and how it will be updated years from now.

Last reviewed: 6 November 2026 · Prepared by: NEXERA İnşaat

SHORT ANSWER

The real value of a smart apartment is the infrastructure installed during construction: enough cabling and empty conduits, a central distribution panel and open standards such as KNX or Matter/Thread. Ask in writing which systems are installed; check that basic functions work locally when the internet is down, where camera and sensor data are stored, whether there are monthly fees and who handles maintenance and updates after handover.

1. Define “smart” first

Ask the seller for a list of every installed system — lighting, blinds, heating and cooling, door and lock, video intercom, security cameras, leak and smoke sensors, energy metering, shared-area and parking access — with brand, model and the app used. “Smart-home ready” is not the same as “installed and working”.

2. Infrastructure matters more than gadgets

  • Cabling: network cable (Cat6 or better) to the rooms, empty conduits, a central communications panel. Devices change every few years; what is inside the walls stays with the building.
  • Open standards: KNX for wired automation; Matter and Thread for wireless devices from different brands — these avoid lock-in to one manufacturer.
  • Coverage: Wi-Fi access points should be planned so every room is covered.

3. What happens when the internet is down?

Lights, heating and the door should keep working locally, without depending on a company’s cloud. Ask how locks and the intercom behave in a power cut.

4. Data and privacy

  • Where are camera, microphone and sensor data stored, who can access them and for how long?
  • How are shared-area cameras managed under Türkiye’s data-protection law (KVKK, Law No. 6698)?
  • Are default passwords changed at handover and do devices receive security updates?

5. Fees, maintenance and warranty

  • Are there monthly or yearly fees, and what stops working if you do not pay?
  • Who maintains the system after handover and whom do you call when something fails?
  • Warranty periods and spare-part availability; make sure administrator access is handed over to you.

6. Ask for at handover

A device list with serial numbers, network and panel diagrams, transfer of user and admin accounts, warranty documents and a short training. When buying off-plan, these items should be written into the sales contract or specification.
