GUIDE · TITLE DEED

Title deed (tapu) checklist before you buy.

A property in Türkiye is only as safe as the records on its title deed (tapu). Run these checks before you pay a deposit or sign anything; problems found afterwards can take years to resolve.

Last reviewed: 6 November 2026 · Prepared by: NEXERA İnşaat

SHORT ANSWER

See the current land-registry record yourself. Confirm that the seller is the registered owner, what share you are buying, and whether there is any mortgage, attachment, annotation (şerh) or declaration (beyan). Ask whether the deed is full condominium (kat mülkiyeti) or construction servitude (kat irtifakı), whether the building has a construction permit and occupancy permit (iskân), and whether any building dues are unpaid. Do not pay most of the price before the title transfer.

1. See the land-registry record yourself

Do not rely on a photocopy. Owners can view their records on the e-Devlet “Tapu Bilgileri Sorgulama” service; ask the seller for this record or for a current extract from the land registry office. Check that the block (ada), parcel and unit number match the flat you are buying.

2. Owner and share

  • Is the seller the registered owner? The name on the deed must match the seller’s ID. If someone sells under a power of attorney, check the original document and that it expressly authorises the sale of this property.
  • Shared ownership: if several people are registered, all of them must take part in the sale. Buying one share is not buying the whole flat.

3. Encumbrances: mortgage, attachment, annotations, declarations

RecordWhat it meansWhat to do
Mortgage (ipotek)The property secures a debt, usually a bank loan.Make removal of the mortgage, or a written plan for removing it, a condition before transfer.
Attachment (haciz)An enforcement record placed because of the owner’s debt.Do not buy until it is lifted.
Annotation (şerh)Registers a right (lease, pre-emption, sales promise, risky-building status) so that it binds third parties.Find out what each annotation is and how it affects you; ask a lawyer if unsure.
Declaration (beyan)Informational notes such as the building’s management plan.Read them, especially any use restrictions in the management plan.

4. Construction servitude or full condominium?

Under the Condominium Law No. 634, kat irtifakı (construction servitude) is established for future independent units in a building that is not finished or has no occupancy permit yet. When the building is completed and the occupancy permit is issued, it is converted into kat mülkiyeti (full condominium ownership). Off-plan purchases normally start with kat irtifakı; if a finished building still has only kat irtifakı, ask why the occupancy permit has not been obtained.

5. Permits and zoning

  • Construction permit (yapı ruhsatı): for a new project, ask for the permit date and number.
  • Occupancy permit (iskân / yapı kullanma izni): for a completed building, see the document. Buildings without it can face problems with utilities, mortgages and resale.
  • Conformity with the approved plans: unapproved changes (enclosed balconies, merged units) can cause problems later.
  • Risky building: a risky-building annotation means the building is in an urban-renewal process under Law No. 6306; assess the consequences separately before buying.

6. Day-to-day debts of the flat

Get written confirmation from the building management that there are no unpaid service charges. Confirm that electricity, water and gas accounts will be transferred without debt, and that compulsory earthquake insurance (DASK) is in force.

7. Tie payment to the transfer

The sale is completed at the land registry office with an official deed. Put any deposit terms in writing, and pay most of the price at the moment of transfer by traceable bank transfer. For off-plan purchases, a notarised sales-promise contract annotated on the title deed protects the buyer against third parties.

This page is general information, not legal, financial or tax advice. Rules and amounts change; confirm the current position with the official authorities and an independent lawyer before any transaction.
