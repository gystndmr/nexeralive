Buying property in Türkiye as a foreigner.

Foreign nationals can buy homes in Türkiye. The purchase is completed at the land registry office in much the same way as for Turkish citizens; the differences are a mandatory valuation report, proof that the price was converted at a Turkish bank (DAB) and a sworn translator if you do not speak Turkish.

Last reviewed: 6 November 2026 · Prepared by: NEXERA İnşaat

SHORT ANSWER

Citizens of countries listed by the President can buy homes, within the statutory area limits and outside military or security zones. You apply through Web Tapu, the Alo 181 line or the land registry office; you need your passport, a compulsory earthquake insurance (DASK) policy, a photo, a valuation report (for foreign buyers) and a foreign-exchange purchase certificate (DAB) issued by a Turkish bank. A sworn translator attends if you do not speak Turkish. The sale is completed with an official deed at the land registry.

Who can buy, and the limits

  • Individuals who are citizens of countries designated by the President may acquire homes and land, subject to legal restrictions. Reciprocity has not been required since 2012.
  • Total foreign ownership may not exceed 10% of the privately owned area of a district, and one person may not hold more than 30 hectares nationwide. Purchases are not possible in military prohibited zones and security zones; land registry offices check this against their own records.
  • Companies incorporated abroad can acquire property only under special-law exceptions; this guide is for individuals.

Step by step

  1. Check the property and its title. Owner, share, mortgage, attachments, annotations, construction and occupancy permits. Our title deed checklist covers this in detail.
  2. Tax number and bank account. In practice foreign buyers are asked for a Turkish tax number, and the payment has to go through a bank in Türkiye.
  3. Application and appointment. Apply through Web Tapu, the Alo 181 call centre or the land registry office.
  4. Valuation report. If the buyer is a foreigner, a report by a valuer licensed by the Capital Markets Board (SPK) is requested through Web Tapu before the sale application.
  5. Foreign-exchange purchase certificate (DAB). Foreign buyers without a Blue Card present a DAB showing that the foreign currency for the price was sold to a bank in Türkiye; the Turkish-lira amount on the DAB is recorded as the sale price.
  6. Documents. Passport, a power of attorney if someone acts for you, a DASK earthquake insurance policy and a photo. If either party does not speak Turkish, a sworn translator from the official register must attend.
  7. Completion day. The title-deed fee and the land registry service fee are paid, the official deed is signed and the title is registered in your name. See our total cost guide for the costs.

Common mistakes

  • Paying a deposit before seeing the title record, or paying most of the price before the transfer.
  • Relying on verbal promises: delivery date, specifications and payment plan must be in writing.
  • Relying only on the seller’s side instead of an independent lawyer and a sworn translator.
  • Assuming that owning property is the same as a residence permit or citizenship: they are separate applications with separate conditions.

Property, residence and citizenship

Owning a home in Türkiye does not by itself give you a residence permit or citizenship. A property-based short-term residence permit and citizenship by investment are separate applications to different authorities with their own conditions (for example, as of 2026 the home must be worth at least USD 200,000 and must not be rented out for a property-based residence permit; citizenship requires a purchase of at least USD 400,000 with a 3-year no-sale annotation). We recommend working with an independent lawyer on these questions.

This page is general information, not legal, financial or tax advice. Rules and amounts change; confirm the current position with the official authorities and an independent lawyer before any transaction.
